# Interview protocol and question bank

Goal: harvest evidence, not adjectives. One question per message. Adapt the wording to what
the imported CV already says ("Your CV says you led the migration to Kubernetes; how many
services moved, and over what period?"). Never ask what the documents already answer.

## Conduct

- Push for a number; accept a range or proxy; record what is confidential (`secret`).
- Ask "before vs after" and "what would have happened without you".
- Separate **I** from **we**: what did the person personally decide or change?
- Ask who could confirm the story (`referee`): a former manager, peer or report.
- Record anything that must not be implied (`do_not_claim`): a project later cancelled, a team
  that did the build, a number that is an estimate.
- Stop probing a story once it has scope, decision, outcome, and evidence or a referee.
- Tag each story with the tracks it serves: `vp`, `cto`, `principal`.

## Phase A — Targeting and storyline (once, revisit when goals change)

1. Which seats, ranked: VP/Head of Engineering (internal, delivery), CTO-track (external,
   vision), Head of Platform/Infrastructure, Principal/Staff IC?
2. Company types and stage: scale-up, PE-backed, enterprise, public sector, a domain? Region
   and CV languages?
3. Non-negotiables: remote, travel, industries they will not work in.
4. For principal: which archetype fits best (Architect, Solver, Right Hand, Tech Lead)?
5. Which two or three accomplishments should every reader remember?
6. Propose the arc you see in the imported history in one or two sentences and ask whether
   it is the story they want to tell. Ask what is missing from it.
7. Name the red flags a recruiter will see (short stints, title versus headcount, gaps, title
   regressions, cancelled flagship work) and ask for the facts behind each.

## Phase B — Role scope (each leadership or Staff+ role)

1. Reporting line: whom did you report to; which leadership team did you sit on?
2. Direct reports and total org: engineers, managers, contractors, vendors; locations.
3. Indirect reach: how many teams or engineers use what your org or system provides?
4. Budget: personnel cost, cloud spend, vendor contracts you owned or influenced; who approved
   what.
5. Systems in scope: services, accounts, clusters, data volume, traffic, uptime targets.
6. State of the org or system when you arrived versus when you left (size, maturity, problems).
7. Why did you join, and why did you leave or move on? (Feeds the storyline and red-flag
   framing; usually not printed.)

## Phase C — Evidence probes by dimension

Ask the dimensions that matter for the person's tracks and storyline; skip those that yield
nothing after one probe.

**Org building and talent**
- How many people did you hire? Any teams built from zero: size, time to first delivery?
- A reorg or team-topology change: what triggered it, what changed, result?
- Managers or engineers you grew into bigger roles? Promotions?
- Attrition or retention numbers; a hard performance case you handled?

**Strategy and decisions**
- A strategic direction you set: what were the options, what did you decide, who disagreed?
- A build-vs-buy, migrate-vs-keep or sunset decision: cost and risk reasoning; outcome.
- A decision you got wrong and what you changed afterwards.
- Work you deliberately stopped or said no to, and what that freed up.

**Delivery and operations**
- Processes you introduced (planning cadence, incident review, on-call, paved paths): measured
  before and after (lead time, deploy frequency, MTTR, incidents, throughput)?
- Biggest incident or reliability problem: your role, what changed structurally?
- Time to onboard a new team, service or engineer, before versus after?

**Financial**
- Spend trend; savings achieved (absolute or %); how (rightsizing, commitments, architecture,
  vendor renegotiation)?
- A unit-cost metric (cost per customer, per request, per environment)?
- Did you build or defend a budget? A business case for headcount or tooling?

**Platform and adoption**
- How many teams or services run on what you built or own? Growth over time?
- Standards or paved paths you defined: adoption, mandated or voluntary?
- Security or compliance outcomes: audits passed, findings reduced, policies automated?

**Cross-functional and executive influence**
- Partners outside engineering (Product, Sales, Security, Finance, Legal, works council,
  customers)?
- Something you got funded or stopped by convincing senior leadership?
- Board, executive staff or group-level reporting you produced?

**Business outcomes and customers (especially `cto`)**
- Revenue, launches, customer wins or retention your work enabled?
- Technical vision presented to board, investors or customers; due diligence; partnerships?

**Transformation and AI**
- Org-level changes you drove (cloud adoption, operating-model change, in-sourcing).
- AI: anything in production or adopted beyond yourself: teams using it, measurable effect,
  cost, guardrails, governance?

**Principal and architecture depth**
- Systems you architected that others built on: scale, lifetime, teams involved, your
  decision rights?
- Technical standards or review gates you owned?
- A knotty problem you were sent into: stuck how long, resolved how?

**Consulting and agency roles**
- Engagement size (people, teams, vendors, budget), duration, your accountability.
- Measured client outcomes: conversion, lead time, migration completed, extensions or renewals
  won.

**Visibility and prestige**
- Talks, writing, open source, community roles, awards, certifications (current, expired, in
  progress). Dates, venues, audience size, links.

## Phase D — Synthesis checks

- Per selected track: at least three complete stories with a number. If not, return to
  Phase C on the weakest dimension.
- Each signature theme is demonstrated by at least two stories in different roles.
- Draft headline and a one- or two-line summary per track; confirm with the person.
- Agree the framing for every red flag.
- One referee per complete story.
